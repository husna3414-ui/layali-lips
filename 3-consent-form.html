Risks and complications

Before undergoing this procedure, it is essential to understand the associated risks clearly. While every effort is made to minimise risks, no method is entirely risk-free. The following risks may occur, and there may also be unforeseen risks not on this list. It has been explained to me that there are inherent and potential risks and side effects in any invasive procedure, and in the case of lip fillers these include, but are not limited to:

Common risks and side effects may include:

  • Localised burning or stinging pain during injection
  • Bruising and redness
  • Tenderness
  • Mild swelling at the injection site
  • Infection at the injection site
  • Feeling faint during the procedure
  • Uneven or inadequate results, which may require additional treatment during a review

Uncommon and rare effects may include:

  • Permanent formation of granuloma, nodules, or lumps that may require surgical correction and could be untreatable
  • Necrosis of the skin
  • Prolonged redness
  • Prolonged sensitivity or pain at the treated sites
  • Scar formation after injection (keloid)
  • Infection leading to scar formation
  • Nerve damage resulting in numbness and weakness
  • Immediate hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis
  • Nasolacrimal duct obstruction or blindness (when treating around the eye area)
  • Vascular occlusion

Additional risks associated with the injection procedure:

  • Trauma during the procedure, including bleeding, bruising, and hematoma
  • Damage to underlying structures such as veins, arteries, nerves, salivary glands, lymph nodes, bone, muscle, and other soft tissue. In rare cases this could cause persistent problems in appearance, sensation, or function, requiring medical intervention or potentially being permanent. Most traumatic injuries heal entirely on their own.
  • Infection from bacterial, viral, or fungal sources
  • Immediate swelling and, very rarely, life-threatening anaphylaxis; delayed reactions causing nodules, lumps, bumps or, very rarely, sterile abscesses, soon after or months later
  • Lumps, bumps, and swellings
  • Skin changes, including pigment changes, thread veins or new capillaries, and other blemishes — which may recover, require further treatment, or be permanent

Vascular occlusion

A vascular occlusion occurs when filler blocks or compresses a blood vessel. Although rare, this is considered a medical emergency. Possible signs include:

  • Severe pain
  • Increasing pain
  • White skin (blanching)
  • Grey, blue or purple discolouration
  • Mottled skin appearance
  • Delayed capillary refill
  • Skin becoming cold
  • Blistering
  • Vision changes

I understand that if these symptoms occur I must contact Layali Clinic immediately (07787 708200) or seek urgent medical attention.

Cold sore reactivation

Lip filler may trigger the reactivation of dormant Herpes Simplex Virus (cold sores). I understand that:

  • A cold sore outbreak may occur after treatment.
  • Antiviral medication may be recommended.
  • Treatment may need to be postponed if a cold sore is present.

Filler migration

Although uncommon, filler may move from its original placement. This may result in:

  • Blurring of the lip border
  • Upper lip fullness above the vermilion border
  • Shape changes

Additional treatment or filler dissolution may be required.

Delayed reactions

Rare delayed reactions may occur weeks, months or years after treatment. These may include swelling, nodules, lumps and inflammatory reactions. Triggers may include illness, infection, dental treatment, vaccination, or immune system activation. Further treatment may be required.

Please confirm to continue.

Contraindications

The following conditions and circumstances may restrict or prohibit the use of fillers: pregnancy or breastfeeding; sports doping testing (the product contains an active compound that may produce a positive reaction); hypersensitivity/allergy to filler components (hyaluronic acid, lidocaine or vitamins) or a history of anaphylaxis/severe allergy; autoimmune diseases; active herpes, acne, rosacea or unhealed skin alterations; complications after surgery within the past five years; previous use of permanent products (silicone, acrylic, polymers, dextran); untreated dental/ENT infection or recent dental abscess; and combining with peeling, laser or ultrasound treatments.

Procedure & benefits

Procedure

  • Hyaluronic acid-based dermal filler is injected into specific areas to enhance the lips and reduce fine lines.
  • Anaesthesia or numbing medicine may or may not be used to minimise discomfort.
  • The treatment site(s) is cleaned with an antiseptic solution.
  • Dermal fillers are transparent gels injected under the skin using a fine needle or cannula.
  • The depth and placement depend on the area and desired result.
  • Multiple injections may be required depending on size, depth and technique.
  • Gentle massage of the injection site may be performed to conform to surrounding tissues.
  • Ice may be applied to reduce swelling if needed.
  • Additional treatments may be necessary to achieve the desired level of correction.
  • Periodic enhancement injections may be required to maintain the desired results.

Benefits

  • Dermal fillers are a safe and effective way to enhance the lips and soften fine lines.
  • Results are immediate and can be built gradually for a natural finish.

Aftercare, day by day

Immediately after the procedure

  • You may experience swelling, bruising, and tenderness in the treated area.
  • For the first 24 hours, apply an ice pack or cold compress (10 minutes on, 10 minutes off) to reduce swelling and discomfort.
  • Avoid touching or applying pressure to the treated area to minimise the risk of infection.

The first 24 to 48 hours

  • Avoid vigorous exercise, hot baths, saunas, steam rooms, and excessive heat or sunlight exposure.
  • Avoid hot or spicy foods and beverages.
  • Drink plenty of water to stay hydrated.

Days 2 to 7

  • Gently cleanse the treated area with a mild cleanser and water twice daily.
  • Apply a thin layer of recommended lip balm or ointment to moisten the lips.
  • Avoid excessive sun exposure and use a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher on your lips.

Weeks 2 to 4

  • The swelling and bruising should gradually subside.
  • Avoid dental procedures, facial massages, or activities that may put pressure on the lips.

Long-term care

  • Protect your lips from excessive sun with an SPF lip balm.
  • Maintain a healthy lifestyle to support overall skin health.
  • Follow any additional instructions from your practitioner.
17

Managing side effects at home

Discomfort & pain: take paracetamol if needed — not ibuprofen or aspirin. Avoid touching or pressing the treated area.

Swelling: apply a wrapped cold compress for 10 minutes with 10-minute breaks; sleep with your head elevated.

Bruising: arnica may help (consult first); avoid blood-thinning medications/supplements for a few days unless medically necessary.

Hydration: drink plenty of water; moisturise with a recommended lip balm; don't pick or peel dry skin.

Sun protection: use a high-SPF (30+) lip balm and sunscreen on the surrounding skin.

Attend any scheduled follow-up appointments and contact us with any concerns.
