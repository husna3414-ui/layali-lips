Risks and complications

Before undergoing this procedure, it is essential to understand the associated risks clearly. While every effort is made to minimise risks, it is necessary to acknowledge that no method is entirely risk-free. The following risks may occur, and there may also be unforeseen risks that are not included on this list. Some risks, if they occur, may require hospitalisation or extended outpatient therapy for adequate treatment. It has been explained to me that there are inherent and potential risks and side effects in any invasive procedure, and in the case of lip fillers, these risks include, but are not limited to:

Common risks and side effects may include:

  • Localised burning or stinging pain during injection
  • Bruising and redness
  • Tenderness
  • Mild swelling at the injection site
  • Infection at the injection site
  • Feeling faint during the procedure
  • Uneven or inadequate results, which may require additional treatment during a review

Uncommon and rare effects may include:

  • Permanent formation of granuloma, nodules, or lumps that may require surgical correction and could be untreatable
  • Necrosis of the skin
  • Prolonged redness
  • Prolonged sensitivity or pain at the treated sites
  • Scar formation after injection (keloid)
  • Infection leading to scar formation
  • Nerve damage resulting in numbness and weakness
  • Immediate hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis
  • Nasolacrimal duct obstruction or blindness (when treating around the eye area)
  • Vascular occlusion

Additional risks associated with the injection procedure:

  • Trauma during the procedure, including bleeding, bruising, and hematoma (a more extensive collection of blood in the skin, outside of blood vessels)
  • Damage to underlying structures such as veins, arteries, nerves, salivary glands, lymph nodes, bone, muscle, and other soft tissue structures. In rare cases, this could cause persistent problems in appearance, sensation, or function, requiring medical intervention or potentially being permanent. Most traumatic injuries heal entirely on their own.

Other risks include:

  • Infection, which bacterial, viral, or fungal sources can cause
  • Reactions rarely occur but can include immediate swelling and, in very rare cases, life-threatening anaphylaxis. Delayed reactions localised to the skin may cause nodules, lumps, bumps, or, very rarely, sterile abscesses. These reactions may occur soon after the procedure or months later.
  • Lumps, bumps, and swellings
  • Skin changes, including pigment changes, the formation of thread veins or new capillaries, and other blemishes. These changes may recover independently, require further treatment, or be permanent.

I have been provided with the necessary information regarding these risks, and any questions or concerns I had have been addressed. I understand the potential complications associated with the lip filler procedure and still wish to proceed with the treatment.

Possible side effects of the filler treatment may include:

  • Dissatisfaction with the aesthetic results
  • Redness, bruising, ecchymosis (discolouration caused by bleeding beneath the skin), hematoma (collection of blood outside of blood vessels), oedema (swelling), itching, and mild pain at the injection site are usually temporary and subside within 24 hours to 8 days, with an average duration of 72 hours.
  • Indurations or nodules may develop at the injection site between 15 days and three months after the procedure.
  • Discolouration of the injection zone.
  • While rare, the medical literature reports cases of necrosis in the glabellar region, abscess, granuloma, and hypersensitivity reactions following hyaluronic acid injections. If you experience any side effects after the treatment, you must contact your practitioner immediately for proper evaluation and management.

Contraindications

Before undergoing the filler treatment, it is essential to be aware of the contraindications and potential side effects. The following conditions and circumstances may restrict or prohibit the use of fillers:

  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding: the treatment is not recommended for pregnant or breastfeeding individuals.
  • Sports doping testing: sports persons should be aware that the product contains an active compound that may produce a positive reaction during doping testing.
  • Hypersensitivity and allergies: inform your practitioner if you have a history of hypersensitivity to any components of the filler (such as hyaluronic acid, lidocaine, or vitamins) or have experienced anaphylactic shock or severe allergies in the past.
  • Autoimmune diseases: individuals with autoimmune diseases or conditions affecting the immune system (such as type 1 diabetes, polyarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, psoriasis, thyroid disorder, scleroderma, inflammatory intestinal disease, lupus, multiple sclerosis, or ulcerative colitis) may not be suitable candidates. Consultation is necessary to assess eligibility.
  • Skin conditions and unhealed alterations: existing pathology such as herpes, acne, rosacea, or unhealed skin alterations may require postponing treatment until resolved.
  • Complications after previous surgery: if you have experienced complications following surgery within the past five years, discuss this with your practitioner.
  • Previous use of permanent products: individuals who have previously received injections of permanent products like silicone, acrylic, polymers, or dextran may not be suitable candidates.
  • Untreated infections: untreated infectious periodontitis, cellulitis, dental or ENT diseases, or untreated/recently treated dental abscesses (less than one week ago) — treatment should be postponed until resolved.
  • Peeling, laser, or ultrasound treatments: combining the filler treatment with peeling, laser, or ultrasound treatments is not recommended.

The filler used has been commercially available for many years, with numerous successful injections. While there are no known unknown risks associated with the product, hyaluronic acid-based products, like any other medical intervention, may have potential side effects. These can vary depending on external factors (type of product, injection technique, site, number of injections, and quantity used) and individual factors (injection tolerance, nervousness, and medical history).

Procedure & benefits

Procedure

  • Hyaluronic acid-based dermal filler is injected into specific face areas to reduce wrinkles and folds.
  • Anaesthesia or numbing medicine may or may not be used to minimise discomfort.
  • The treatment site(s) is cleaned with an antiseptic solution.
  • Dermal fillers are transparent gels injected under the skin using a thin needle.
  • The depth of injection depends on the depth and location of the wrinkle(s).
  • Multiple injections may be required depending on size, depth, and technique.
  • Gentle massage of the injection site may be performed to conform to surrounding tissues.
  • Ice may be applied to reduce swelling if needed.
  • Additional treatments may be necessary to achieve the desired level of correction.
  • Periodic enhancement injections may be required to maintain the desired results.

Benefits

  • Compared to other products, dermal fillers are safe and effective for filling face wrinkles, lines, and folds.
  • The effects can last six months or longer without the need for re-administration.

Aftercare, day by day

Immediately after the procedure

  • You may experience swelling, bruising, and tenderness in the treated area.
  • For the first 24 hours, apply an ice pack or cold compress (10 minutes on, 10 minutes off) to reduce swelling and discomfort.
  • Avoid touching or applying pressure to the treated area to minimise the risk of infection.

The first 24 to 48 hours

  • Avoid vigorous exercise, hot baths, saunas, steam rooms, and excessive heat or sunlight exposure.
  • Avoid consuming hot or spicy foods and beverages.
  • Drink plenty of water to stay hydrated.

Days 2 to 7

  • Gently cleanse the treated area with a mild cleanser and water twice daily.
  • Apply a thin layer of prescribed or recommended lip balm or ointment to moisten the lips.
  • Avoid excessive sun exposure and use a broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher on your lips.

Weeks 2 to 4

  • The swelling and bruising should gradually subside during this time.
  • Avoid dental procedures, facial massages, or activities that may pressure the lips.

Long-term care

  • Protect your lips from excessive sun exposure by wearing a lip balm with SPF whenever you go outside.
  • Maintain a healthy lifestyle, including a balanced diet and regular exercise, to support overall skin health.
  • Follow the instructions provided by your healthcare provider for any additional post-treatment care.

Managing side effects at home

Discomfort and pain

To help alleviate discomfort, take over-the-counter pain relievers such as paracetamol — not ibuprofen or aspirin — as directed by the packaging instructions. Avoid touching or pressing the treated area to prevent additional pain or irritation.

Swelling

Apply a cold compress or ice pack wrapped in a clean cloth to the treated area for 10 minutes, with 10-minute breaks. Sleeping with an extra pillow or elevating your head while resting can also help minimise swelling.

Bruising

Arnica gel or cream applied topically may help reduce the appearance of bruising — consult your healthcare provider before using topical products and follow their instructions. Avoid taking blood-thinning medications or supplements (if not medically necessary) for a few days after the procedure, as they can increase the risk of bruising.

Hydration and moisturisation

Drink plenty of water to stay hydrated, as it aids healing and can help minimise dryness. Apply a thin layer of lip balm or ointment recommended by your healthcare provider to moisturise your lips. Avoid picking or peeling any dry skin on the lips.

Sun protection

When going outside, use a lip balm with a high SPF (30 or higher) to protect your lips and the surrounding skin from excessive sun exposure. Apply sunscreen to the surrounding skin if your lips will be exposed to direct sunlight.

Follow the healthcare provider's instructions

Adhere to any specific post-treatment instructions provided. Attend any scheduled follow-up appointments to monitor your progress and address any concerns.
