Before Your Treatment

Preparing for Lip Filler

A little preparation helps reduce bruising and swelling and gives you the loveliest, smoothest result.

Lips bruise and swell easily, so these simple steps make a real difference to your comfort and your result. If anything is unclear, just ask us before your appointment.

Important: never stop prescribed medication without first speaking to your GP or prescriber.

Your preparation timeline

1 week before

Where appropriate, avoid blood-thinning medicines & supplements (see below). If you're prone to cold sores, tell us — you may need antiviral medication. Avoid other lip treatments.

48 hours before

No alcohol. Avoid intense exercise. Keep well hydrated.

On the day

Eat beforehand, arrive with clean lips (no lipstick/makeup), and plan a quiet couple of weeks around any big events.

To reduce bruising — about 1 week before

Why: these thin the blood and make lips bruise more. Only where appropriate, and never stop anything prescribed without your doctor's approval.

Medicines

  • Aspirin
  • Ibuprofen & anti-inflammatories
  • Blood thinners (only with your doctor's agreement)

Supplements

  • Fish oil & Omega-3
  • Vitamin E
  • Ginkgo biloba
  • High-dose garlic / ginger

Paracetamol is fine if you need pain relief. Arnica beforehand may also help.

Also worth knowing

Please avoid lip filler if

  • You have an active cold sore or any lip/mouth infection
  • You have a holiday, wedding or big event within ~2 weeks
  • You have recent or upcoming dental work (leave ~2 weeks either side)
  • You feel unwell or are run down

On the day, please do

  • Eat a meal and drink water
  • Arrive with clean, product-free lips
  • Allow time so you're relaxed
  • Plan to take it easy afterwards

Please tell us before your appointment if you

  • Are prone to cold sores (you may need antiviral medication first)
  • Feel unwell or have an infection
  • Are pregnant or think you might be
  • Have started any new medication
  • Have had recent dental treatment
If in doubt, it's always safer to call us — we may simply rebook you to a better time.
